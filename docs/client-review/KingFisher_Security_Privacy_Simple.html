Section 02

How passwords are protected

When a password is created or changed, KingFisher Wings does not retain the typed value. The system converts it into a one-way scramble and stores only that result.

Plain analogy: Fruit placed in a blender cannot be restored as whole fruit. Password protection works in the same spirit — the stored form cannot be turned back into the original password.
1
Password entered Login or setup screen
→
2
One-way scramble Industry-standard method
→
3
Scramble stored Original value discarded
→
4
Checked at login New entry compared safely
Illustrative example (fictional account)

Value typed by the user

Welcome@123

Value retained by the system

$argon2id$v=19$m=65536,t=3,p=1$fscbKfXqzPsE+/5mtcKqDw$kYIllLFe2ETG10f7GpHPT11jkD5V0r6o2MptmoYjRSQ

The long stored string is a fingerprint of the password — not a code that can be typed to gain access.

Practice we reject

Saving Welcome@123 in readable form. Anyone with a copy of the storage could read every password.

Practice we apply

Saving only the scrambled fingerprint. Even a full storage copy does not reveal the original password.

Client assurance: Support personnel and system administrators cannot retrieve a user’s original password. Forgotten passwords must be reset; they cannot be recovered.

Section 04

Your organisation’s data remains yours

Multiple organisations may use KingFisher Wings concurrently — comparable to separate offices in one building. Each organisation holds its own key. Neighbouring organisations cannot open your door.

Strict separation — Organisation A cannot view Organisation B’s jobs or customers

Organisation A may access

  • Its own jobs and shipments
  • Its own customers and invoices
  • Its own staff accounts

Organisation B may access

  • Only its own jobs and shipments
  • Only its own customers and invoices
  • Only its own staff accounts
Worked example: A user from Gulf Freight opens the Jobs list. Only Gulf Freight records appear. Asia Lines shipments do not appear on screen, in exports or in search — even though both organisations use the same product.

Two independent safeguards

Safeguard Purpose
Application control Every request is bound to the signed-in organisation before data is retrieved.
Storage control The data store itself refuses rows belonging to another organisation — a second line of defence.

Section 05

Separate entrances for separate roles

Different parties do not share a single entrance. This prevents a customer session from behaving as a staff session, and prevents a vendor from viewing another customer’s invoices.

Party Entrance Authorised scope
Platform administrator Dedicated admin sign-in Platform companies and tenants — not day-to-day freight work inside a client organisation
Your staff Staff / company sign-in Operational modules according to role (sales, operations, finance, branch management)
Your customers Customer portal Their own shipments, quotations and invoices — not your internal cost figures
Your vendors Vendor portal Their own supplier documents — kept separate from the customer portal

Session control

  • Sessions may be cancelled immediately on logout
  • Password changes invalidate prior sessions
  • Long-lived credentials are stored in scrambled form

Additional controls

  • Repeated failed attempts are slowed or locked
  • Roles restrict who may approve, edit or settle
  • Optional two-step verification available at go-live

Privacy and safety in daily operations

Outside the customer’s view

  • Internal cost and margin figures
  • Other customers’ records
  • Internal staff notes not intended for release

Behaviours we restrict

  • Automated guessing of passwords
  • Flooding the service with excessive requests
  • Staff actions beyond assigned authority

A typical request — controlled path

  1. Secure arrival — the connection is encrypted in transit (browser padlock).
  2. Identity confirmed — a valid sign-in is required; excessive attempts are constrained.
  3. Authority checked — confirmation that the person may perform the requested action.
  4. Organisation boundary enforced — only that organisation’s records are opened.
  5. Accountability retained — material changes remain available for later review.

Section 08

Database, files & how privacy is enforced

Behind the screens, your information lives in a shared platform building — but each company’s rooms are locked. Privacy is enforced in more than one place so a single mistake cannot expose another company.

Where your data lives

Business database

  • Jobs, invoices, parties, users and finance sit in structured storage
  • Every business row is tagged to one organisation
  • The storage engine itself refuses to return another organisation’s rows
  • Application requests always declare “which company is asking” before reading or writing

Files & generated documents

  • Uploaded proofs, attachments and generated PDFs stay under your organisation
  • Download links respect sign-in and permissions
  • Customer/vendor portals only receive documents they are allowed to see
  • Public tracking uses limited tokens — not open access to your full records

How separation works (non-technical)

1
You sign in Company identity known
→
2
Request tagged “Only this company”
→
3
Storage filter Other companies blocked
→
4
Your data only Screen / export / report
Privacy question Answer
Can Company B query Company A’s database rows? No. Storage rules and application tags both block it.
Can Company B see Company A’s uploaded files? No. Files are resolved only inside the signed-in organisation and rights.
Can a customer see another customer of the same forwarder? No. Portal users see only their own party’s allowed records.
Can a vendor see customer invoices or your margins? No. Vendor entrance is separate; internal cost/GP is stripped.
Are deleted records wiped for everyone immediately? Business records are typically soft-removed (hidden from normal use) so your audit trail remains trustworthy — not handed to other companies.
Do backups mix companies into one open list? Backups protect the platform; restored data still carries organisation tags and the same separation rules.
What can platform administrators see? Platform administration manages companies and tenants — not day-to-day freight operations inside your vault as if they were your staff.
Picture: One library building, many locked cabinets. Staff from Cabinet A cannot open Cabinet B — even though both cabinets stand in the same building.

Independent security certifications

Established software providers (including Fresa Technologies) publish certificates issued after an independent audit of company processes — policies, training, incident handling and operational discipline — in addition to product controls.

Primary market ask

Information security

Confirms an international management system for protecting information (commonly known in the market as ISO 27001).

Quality

Quality management

Demonstrates consistent delivery and continual improvement (commonly known as ISO 9001).

Where AI applies

Responsible AI governance

Formal oversight for artificial intelligence features, as promoted by peers such as Fresa.

How such certificates are obtained

  1. Define scope — which product and services the certificate will cover.
  2. Establish the rulebook — security policy, ownership, risk handling.
  3. Align rules with live controls — password protection, organisation separation, backups and logging (strong technical foundations already exist).
  4. Operate with evidence — retain records over a period (reviews, training, incidents).
  5. Internal assessment — identify and close gaps.
  6. Engage an accredited auditor — review of documentation and practical operation.
  7. Certification — award of the certificate, typically with annual surveillance.
Formal position: KingFisher Wings already applies the technical protections described in this briefing. An official international certificate is an organisational programme that can be initiated when required by contract or market expectation. The customary first target is the information-security certificate (ISO 27001).
